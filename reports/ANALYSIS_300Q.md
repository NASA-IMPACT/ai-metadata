# Analysis — run `20260819T183313Z` (300-query slice)

The 300-query extension of the reference 50-query study. Three models × six
formats × two payloads, retrieval replayed from the same two Stage R runs, all
answers graded by one fixed judge (`gpt-5.4-nano`, temperature 0). **All three

## 3. The payload axis — the one large, unambiguous effect

Paired on (query, format, model); 95% t-intervals.

| comparison | correctness | n |
|---|---|---:|
| faceted − unfaceted, **all models** | **+0.045 [+0.038, +0.052]** | 5,400 |
| … gpt-5.4-nano | +0.049 [+0.036, +0.061] | 1,800 |
| … gpt-5.4-mini | +0.051 [+0.039, +0.063] | 1,800 |
| … muse-glimmer | +0.036 [+0.023, +0.049] | 1,800 |

And it costs **16,533 fewer prompt tokens per query** [−16,870, −16,197] — a
60% reduction, essentially identical across models because the renderings are
shared.

**The curated projection is better and cheaper, for every model tested.** This
is the finding the study was built to test, and it now holds at n=300 in all
three arms, glimmer included — its interval no longer grazes zero the way the
50-query slice's did (+0.038 [+0.004, +0.071] then; +0.036 [+0.023, +0.049]
now).

Faithfulness moves the other way, barely: −0.006 [−0.012, −0.001], and only the
nano arm separates on its own (−0.016). Raw records give the model more text to
stay grounded in, at 2.5× the token cost and four to five points of
correctness. Not a trade worth making.

## 4. The model axis — and a judge problem

Paired on (query, format, payload); every comparison now covers the full 3,600
common cells.

| comparison | correctness | n |
|---|---|---:|
| mini − nano | **−0.076 [−0.082, −0.070]** | 3,600 |
| glimmer − nano | **+0.022 [+0.016, +0.029]** | 3,600 |
| mini − glimmer | **−0.098 [−0.105, −0.091]** | 3,600 |

Read literally: the small cloud model beats the larger one, and a local 32B
beats both. Before accepting that, note **the judge is `gpt-5.4-nano`, which is
also one of the models under test.** It grades its own output in two of these
three comparisons, and in both it comes out ahead of mini. Self-preference is
the leading explanation and this run cannot separate it from genuine quality.

The exception is informative. **`mini − glimmer` is the only judge-neutral
comparison** — neither model is the judge — and it is the *largest* effect in
the table. Likewise `glimmer − nano` is biased *toward* nano, so glimmer's
+0.022 win is a conservative estimate. The two comparisons that don't flatter
the judge both favour the local model.

Worth flagging: glimmer's edge over nano **shrank from +0.067 on the 50-query
slice to +0.022 at n=300**. The early slice was 22% SME queries — glimmer's
best terrain (§6) — so the preliminary number overstated the gap. The full-set
number is the one to quote.

Faithfulness now separates glimmer too: glimmer − nano +0.025 [+0.018, +0.032]
and mini − glimmer −0.029 [−0.035, −0.022], while mini − nano stays at zero
(−0.003 [−0.011, +0.004]). The local model is both the most correct and the
most grounded arm under this judge.

**What this does not license:** any claim that mini is worse than nano. That
comparison is the most bias-exposed one here. Re-judging a slice under a
neutral judge (mini or glimmer) is the obvious next step — `judge_stage.py`
already supports it, and rows carry judge identity so both passes coexist.

## 5. The format axis — real, small, and model-dependent

Paired against JSON, pooled over payloads and models (n=1,800 each):

| format | correctness vs json | n |
|---|---|---:|
| mat | +0.009 [−0.003, +0.020] | 1,800 |
| toon | −0.008 [−0.019, +0.003] | 1,800 |
| yaml | −0.011 [−0.022, −0.000] | 1,800 |
| jsonld | −0.015 [−0.027, −0.003] | 1,800 |
| csv | −0.027 [−0.039, −0.015] | 1,800 |

Only csv and jsonld clearly separate from JSON (yaml touches zero), and by less
than two-thirds of the payload effect. **Format matters far less than what you
put in it.**

The best format is nonetheless model- and payload-dependent, which is what
`Report.md` predicted:

| model | faceted winner | unfaceted winner |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-5.4-nano | yaml (0.850) | json (0.811) |
| gpt-5.4-mini | mat (0.782) | json (0.740) |
| muse-glimmer | toon (0.864) | json (0.837) |

**Under the raw record, JSON wins for all three models** — its explicit
structure appears to help when there are 398 paths to navigate. Under the
curated projection the winner splits three ways, and the within-model spreads
(0.028–0.040) overlap the confidence intervals, so treat the ordering as
suggestive, not established.

Practical note: **`mat` is simultaneously the cheapest format and top-two on
correctness under the faceted payload for all three models** (9,146–9,334
tokens vs 12,470–12,833 for jsonld). It remains the best Pareto point in the
run.

## 6. SME vs synthetic

Correctness, faceted payload, range across the six formats:

| model | SME (n=11) | synthetic (n=289) | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gpt-5.4-nano | 0.364–0.482 | 0.840–0.866 | ~0.43 |
| gpt-5.4-mini | 0.282–0.418 | 0.759–0.799 | ~0.40 |
| muse-glimmer | 0.473–0.627 | 0.853–0.874 | ~0.31 |

Expert-written questions are dramatically harder than questions generated from
the records they retrieve — the synthetic queries inherit vocabulary from their
source record, and the SME ones do not. **Pooled means are dominated by the
SME share**, which is why the pipeline's `sme_share_note` insists on per-slice
comparison.

Glimmer has the smallest SME/synthetic gap of the three and is the best SME
performer, and that now holds on the full arm, not just the 50-query preview.
The SME slice is still only 11 queries, so the ranges are wide.

## 7. Retrieval-side metrics

`contextual_relevancy` is 0.07–0.18 everywhere it was judged and is not a
quality signal here: with 1–2 relevant records among 10 retrieved, it measures
retrieval precision, which Stage R already measures exactly. It is excluded
from the headline metrics.

`contextual_recall` runs counter to correctness — `faceted|mat|nano` scores
0.361 against `unfaceted|jsonld|nano`'s 0.603, while beating it on correctness
0.847 to 0.796. The projection drops fields the judge then cannot find the
answer's claims in, without those fields being needed to answer. Another reason
to treat correctness as the end-to-end metric and the rest as diagnostics.

Both metrics exist for the full nano arm but only the 50-query glimmer subset
and not at all for mini (§0).

## 8. Caveats

1. **The judge is one of the models under test.** See §4. This is now the
   run's dominant caveat.
2. **Diagnostic-metric coverage is uneven** (§0): answer_relevancy and the two
   contextual metrics cover nano fully, glimmer only on the 50-query subset,
   and mini not at all. Correctness and faithfulness — everything §3–§6 uses —
   cover all 10,800 cells.
3. **`cost_usd` is null throughout `answers.jsonl`** — neither model is in
   `data/prices.json`. Dollar figures here come from `analyze_50q.py`'s own
   table (nano $0.20/$1.25, mini $0.75/$4.50 per M, verified 2026-08-19).
4. **Format rankings within a model are not statistically separated.** Only the
   pooled csv and jsonld penalties clear their intervals.

## 9. What to do next

1. **Re-judge under a neutral judge.** The model axis is the run's most
   interesting result and its least trustworthy one; mini or glimmer as judge
   on a slice would bound the self-preference effect.
2. **Optionally backfill the diagnostic metrics** (glimmer's remaining 3,000
   cells and mini's 3,600) if the relevancy/recall diagnostics are wanted at
   full n — the Stage J checkpoint only skips fully-judged metric sets when
   re-run with the wider `--metrics` list.
3. **Ship faceted.** +0.045 correctness at 40% of the token cost, consistent
   across three models at n=300, is the actionable finding and it is not close.
