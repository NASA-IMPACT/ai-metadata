# Analysis — run `20260819T183313Z` (300-query slice)

The 300-query extension of the reference 50-query study. Three models × six
formats × two payloads, retrieval replayed from the same two Stage R runs, all
answers graded by one fixed judge (`gpt-5.4-nano`, temperature 0).

Companion documents: [`STAGED_PIPELINE.md`](STAGED_PIPELINE.md) (how the run is

## 0. Read this first — the arms are not the same size

**The `muse-glimmer` arm covers 50 queries per cell; the two OpenAI arms cover
300.** Stage A for the local model is still in progress (1,243 of 3,600 cells
answered), and Stage J has graded 600 of those. The unpaired per-arm table in
§2 therefore mixes n=300 and n=50 rows, and **its glimmer rows must not be read
against its nano/mini rows.**

This is not a small correction. The 11 SME queries are 22% of the 50-query
slice but 3.7% of the 300-query slice, and SME queries score roughly 0.4
correctness below synthetic ones (§5). The slice difference alone moves
Recall@10 for `faceted|csv` from 0.863 (300 queries) to 0.736 (50 queries) —
retrieval is model-independent, so that entire gap is query mix.

Every comparison in §3 and §4 is **paired** on the common queries, which
restricts glimmer comparisons to the 50-query intersection automatically. Those
are the numbers to quote.

## 3. The payload axis — the one large, unambiguous effect

Paired on (query, format, model); 95% t-intervals.

| comparison | correctness | n |
|---|---|---:|
| faceted − unfaceted, **all models** | **+0.049 [+0.041, +0.058]** | 3,900 |
| … gpt-5.4-nano | +0.049 [+0.036, +0.061] | 1,800 |
| … gpt-5.4-mini | +0.051 [+0.039, +0.063] | 1,800 |
| … muse-glimmer | +0.038 [+0.004, +0.071] | 300 |

And it costs **16,575 fewer prompt tokens per query** [−16,978, −16,172] — a
61% reduction, identical across models because the renderings are shared.

**The curated projection is better and cheaper, for every model tested.** This
is the finding the study was built to test, and it replicates at n=300 with a
tight interval. Nothing else in the run is close to this size.

Faithfulness moves the other way, very slightly: −0.010 [−0.017, −0.002]. Raw
records give the model more text to stay grounded in, at four times the token
cost and five points of correctness. Not a trade worth making.

## 4. The model axis — and a judge problem

Paired on (query, format, payload). Glimmer comparisons use the common
50-query intersection.

| comparison | correctness | n |
|---|---|---:|
| mini − nano | **−0.076 [−0.082, −0.070]** | 3,600 |
| glimmer − nano | **+0.067 [+0.045, +0.088]** | 600 |
| mini − glimmer | **−0.114 [−0.134, −0.094]** | 600 |

Read literally: the small cloud model beats the larger one, and a local 32B
beats both. Before accepting that, note **the judge is `gpt-5.4-nano`, which is
also one of the models under test.** It grades its own output in two of these
three comparisons, and in both it comes out ahead. Self-preference is the
leading explanation and this run cannot separate it from genuine quality.

The exception is informative. **`mini − glimmer` is the only judge-neutral
comparison** — neither model is the judge — and it is the *largest* effect in
the table. Likewise `glimmer − nano` is biased *toward* nano, so glimmer's
+0.067 win is a conservative estimate. The two comparisons that don't flatter
the judge both favour the local model.

**What this does not license:** any claim that mini is worse than nano. That
comparison is the most bias-exposed one here. Re-judging a slice under a
neutral judge (mini or glimmer) is the obvious next step — `judge_stage.py`
already supports it, and rows carry judge identity so both passes coexist.

Faithfulness separates none of the models: every interval crosses zero.

## 5. The format axis — real, small, and model-dependent

Paired against JSON, pooled over payloads and models:

| format | correctness vs json | n |
|---|---|---:|
| mat | +0.008 [−0.005, +0.022] | 1,300 |
| yaml | −0.012 [−0.025, +0.001] | 1,300 |
| toon | −0.012 [−0.025, +0.001] | 1,300 |
| jsonld | −0.020 [−0.034, −0.005] | 1,300 |
| csv | −0.029 [−0.042, −0.016] | 1,300 |

Only jsonld and csv separate from JSON, and by less than a third of the payload
effect. **Format matters far less than what you put in it.**

The best format is nonetheless model- and payload-dependent, which is what
`Report.md` predicted:

| model | faceted winner | unfaceted winner |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-5.4-nano | yaml (0.850) | json (0.811) |
| gpt-5.4-mini | mat (0.782) | json (0.740) |
| muse-glimmer | mat (0.826) | json (0.786) |

**Under the raw record, JSON wins for all three models** — its explicit
structure appears to help when there are 398 paths to navigate. Under the
curated projection the winner splits, and prose (`mat`) leads for two of three.
The within-model spreads (0.026–0.066) overlap the confidence intervals, so
treat the ordering as suggestive, not established.

Practical note: **`mat` is simultaneously the cheapest format and top-two on
correctness under the faceted payload** (9,334 tokens vs 12,833 for jsonld). It
is the best Pareto point in the run.

## 6. SME vs synthetic

Correctness, faceted payload:

| model | SME (n=11) | synthetic | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| gpt-5.4-nano | 0.364–0.482 | 0.840–0.866 | ~0.43 |
| gpt-5.4-mini | 0.282–0.418 | 0.759–0.799 | ~0.40 |
| muse-glimmer | 0.473–0.627 | 0.833–0.908 | ~0.31 |

Expert-written questions are dramatically harder than questions generated from
the records they retrieve — the synthetic queries inherit vocabulary from their
source record, and the SME ones do not. **Pooled means are dominated by the
SME share**, which is why §0's warning about the 50/300 mismatch matters and
why the pipeline's own `sme_share_note` insists on per-slice comparison.

Glimmer has the smallest SME/synthetic gap of the three. On the 50-query slice
it is also the best SME performer. Worth revisiting once its Stage A completes.

## 7. Retrieval-side metrics

`contextual_relevancy` is 0.07–0.15 everywhere and is not a quality signal
here: with 1–2 relevant records among 10 retrieved, it measures retrieval
precision, which Stage R already measures exactly. It is excluded from the
headline metrics.

`contextual_recall` runs counter to correctness — `faceted|mat|nano` scores
0.361 against `unfaceted|jsonld|nano`'s 0.603, while beating it on correctness
0.847 to 0.796. The projection drops fields the judge then cannot find the
answer's claims in, without those fields being needed to answer. Another reason
to treat correctness as the end-to-end metric and the rest as diagnostics.

## 8. Caveats

1. **The glimmer arm is incomplete** — 1,243/3,600 answered, 600 judged, plus
   **643 answered cells not yet judged**. Re-run Stage J after Stage A
   finishes; the checkpoint will pick up only the new cells.
2. **The judge is one of the models under test.** See §4. `judge_summary.json`
   records this as `self_judge_models`.
3. **Unpaired arm means mix slice sizes.** §2 rows at n=50 and n=300 are not
   comparable; §3–§5 pairings are.
4. **`cost_usd` is null throughout `answers.jsonl`** — neither model is in
   `data/prices.json`. Dollar figures here come from `analyze_50q.py`'s own
   table (nano $0.20/$1.25, mini $0.75/$4.50 per M, verified 2026-08-19).
5. **Format rankings within a model are not statistically separated.** Only the
   pooled csv and jsonld penalties clear their intervals.
